# G3_01 -- Ciccarese, Soiland-Reyes, Belhajjame, Gray, Goble & Clark (2013)

**PAV ontology: provenance, authoring and versioning.** Journal of Biomedical Semantics, 4, 37. DOI: 10.1186/2041-1480-4-37. Zotero key: TXRF6GUC. Domain: biomedical semantics / general-purpose Semantic Web provenance (NOT construction/BIM).

**Source read:** Full text, complete (`G3_TXRF6GUC_PAVOntology.txt`, 22 pages, through the Conclusions and Discussion/BIBFRAME/PRV comparison sections).

---

**Q1 -- Research problem.** Existing general-purpose provenance vocabularies (Dublin Core Terms, PROV-O, Open Provenance Model, Provenance Vocabulary) either conflate distinct roles of content origination (author/curator/creator/contributor) or are too verbose/process-oriented for lightweight, "just enough" tracking of who did what to a digital resource and when. PAV (Provenance, Authoring, and Versioning) proposes a compact, reusable vocabulary that explicitly distinguishes these roles and maps cleanly onto PROV-O.

**Q2 -- Information architecture.** NOT_APPLICABLE in Paper 2's Source->BIM->Record chain sense. PAV's "chain" is: original knowledge source (e.g. a scientific publication) -> author (`pav:authoredBy`) -> curator (`pav:curatedBy`, who reformats/restructures the content) -> artifact creator (`pav:createdBy`, who physically encodes it as a digital resource) -> versioned digital resource (`pav:version`, `pav:previousVersion`, `pav:derivedFrom`). This is a *role/attribution* chain, not a component-identity chain, but it is directly reusable as the "who" layer for Paper 2's inspection-record accountability.

**Q3 -- Identity representation.** NOT_APPLICABLE. PAV does not address physical/BIM-component identity; it addresses digital-*resource* (document/dataset/statement) identity via URIs and `pav:version` strings.

**Q4 -- Identity persistence across model revisions -> "Versioning and evolution."** YES (directly transferable concept, though for documents not BIM components). CLAIM: PAV adopts a "snapshot"-based versioning approach: `pav:version` (free-text version identifier) and `pav:previousVersion` (links a version to its immediate predecessor "of the same lineage") for minor/linear revisions, while `pav:derivedFrom` is used when content has been "further refined or modified" such that the result "no longer share[s] lineage" with the original -- a distinction between same-identity-different-state (previousVersion) and different-identity-related-content (derivedFrom). EVIDENCE: Table 3 and Figure 4 (Hypothesis A1 -> A1' via `pav:lastUpdateOn`, and A1 -> A2 via `pav:previousVersion`, contrasted with Hypothesis B `pav:derivedFrom` A1 at version 0.1). IMPLICATION: this same-identity-vs-different-identity distinction is directly relevant conceptual machinery Paper 2 could reuse or cite when defining what counts as "the same BIM component across a model revision" versus "a genuinely new/derived component."

**Q5a -- Source provenance.** YES, directly and precisely modeled. `pav:providedBy` (original external provider, e.g. PubMed/UniProt, distinct from the current `dct:publisher`), `pav:retrievedFrom`/`pav:retrievedBy`/`pav:retrievedOn` (unmodified retrieval/caching of a resource), `pav:importedFrom`/`pav:importedBy`/`pav:importedOn` (retrieval *with* a format/representation transformation), `pav:sourceAccessedAt`/`pav:sourceAccessedBy`/`pav:sourceAccessedOn` (a source merely consulted, not retrieved or derived from).

**Q5b -- Measurement provenance.** NOT_APPLICABLE -- PAV is not sensor/measurement-oriented; it is document/knowledge-artifact-oriented.

**Q5c -- Processing provenance.** PARTIAL/YES. `pav:importedFrom` explicitly captures "content has been preserved, but transcribed somehow, for instance to fit a different representation model by converting formats" -- a processing-transformation provenance link, distinct from unmodified retrieval.

**Q5d -- Human-actor provenance.** YES, this is PAV's central and most directly relevant contribution to Paper 2. CLAIM: PAV explicitly distinguishes four contributor sub-roles with clear, non-overlapping definitions: `pav:authoredBy` (originated/gave existence to the *content*), `pav:curatedBy` (a content specialist who shapes/reformats the expression, "often the primary agent responsible for ensuring the quality of the representation" -- explicitly may be a software agent, e.g. text-mining software), `pav:createdBy` (the agent who physically encoded the digital *representation*, distinct from who originated the content), and `pav:contributedBy` (the generic superproperty covering all of the above plus other help). EVIDENCE: Table 1 (authoring properties) and Table 2 (provenance properties), with a worked example (Figure 2: content authored by "Golde T.", curated by "Wong G.", digital artifact created by "Wu E.," published by "AlzSWAN Team"). IMPLICATION: PAV's author/curator/creator distinction is directly analogous to Paper 2's G3 need to distinguish performer/reviewer/approver roles on an inspection record -- PAV shows this is a *solved, standardized, reusable* problem in the general Semantic Web provenance literature, though not previously applied to BIM inspection records specifically (per this targeted review's other findings).

**Q5e -- Review/approval provenance.** PARTIAL. PAV's `pav:curatedBy` role ("a content specialist responsible for shaping the expression in an appropriate format... often the primary agent responsible for ensuring the quality of the representation") functions similarly to a quality-review role, but PAV does not define an explicit approval/sign-off state machine (e.g. draft -> submitted -> approved) or a superseded-record marker beyond `pav:previousVersion`/`pav:derivedFrom`. LIMITATION: PAV is explicitly "lightweight" by design and defers detailed role/attribution-event modeling (e.g. exact timestamps per individual author when there are multiple authors) to a companion PROV-O trace (Fig. 5), not to PAV itself.

**Q6 -- Model/version context.** YES, see Q4 -- PAV's snapshot-based versioning is exactly a model/version-context mechanism, generalized from documents to any digital resource.

**Q7 -- Validity/applicability scope.** YES, explicit. PAV states its scope precisely: "we do not attempt to model the whole chain of process-oriented provenance like Open Provenance Model (OPM) processes and PROV-O activities... Rather, in PAV we focus on the provenance of a digital resource in terms of its relationships with other digital resources and agents." Five adopting projects/communities are named (SWAN/AlzSWAN, Annotation Ontology/Domeo, Nanopublications, Open PHACTS, Wf4Ever Research Objects, Elsevier Satellite), demonstrating cross-domain reuse, though none in construction/BIM.

**Q8 -- Forward and reverse traceability.** PARTIAL. `pav:previousVersion` and `pav:derivedFrom` allow reverse traversal from a resource to its lineage predecessor(s); no explicit forward-traceability (predecessor-to-successor enumeration) query pattern is presented, though it is structurally possible given the RDF representation.

**Q9 -- Validation method.** PARTIAL/YES, but methodologically different from an empirical case study. PAV's "validation" is (a) real-world adoption evidence across five independent projects/communities with worked examples from each, and (b) a formal interoperability demonstration mapping PAV properties as `PROV-O` subproperties, executed via a Taverna workflow using the Pellet OWL reasoner and visualized with ProvToolbox on a real Open PHACTS/ChemSpider VoID dataset (Figure 14) -- confirming that PROV-O statements can be correctly inferred from asserted PAV statements.

**Q10 -- Deliberate invalid-case testing.** NOT_REPORTED. No discussion of malformed/orphaned/conflicting provenance-record testing.

**Q11 -- G3 record fields (performer/reviewer/approver/timestamp/status/superseded-record/evidence-source) -- mapped to PAV's closest analogues.** performer -> PARTIAL (`pav:authoredBy`/`pav:createdBy`, but for content/artifact origination, not "who performed an inspection" specifically); reviewer -> PARTIAL (`pav:curatedBy`, quality-shaping role, not a formal review/approval workflow); approver -> NO (no dedicated approval-state concept); timestamp -> YES (`pav:authoredOn`, `pav:curatedOn`, `pav:createdOn`, `pav:importedOn`, etc., one per role); status -> NO (no explicit status enumeration beyond version identifiers); superseded-record -> YES (`pav:previousVersion` explicitly marks lineage supersession); evidence-source -> YES (`pav:providedBy`, `pav:retrievedFrom`, `pav:sourceAccessedAt` collectively cover this).

**Q12 -- ALREADY_ESTABLISHED vs REMAINING_GAP.** ALREADY_ESTABLISHED: a compact, reusable, PROV-O-compatible vocabulary distinguishing author/curator/creator/contributor roles with per-role timestamps, source/import/retrieval provenance, and snapshot-based versioning with explicit same-lineage-vs-derived distinction -- a mature (published 2013, now version 2.2), cross-domain-adopted general solution to *most* of what Paper 2's G3 accountability layer needs conceptually. REMAINING_GAP, critical for Paper 2's positioning: (1) PAV has never been applied to BIM/construction inspection records (confirmed absent from this entire targeted corpus's other 11 papers); (2) PAV lacks a formal approval/sign-off state machine and explicit "status" field, which Paper 2's inspection-record accountability likely needs; (3) PAV is document/resource-centric, not physical-asset-component-centric, so it does not itself solve the BIM-GUID identity-persistence problem -- it would need to be paired with (not replace) a component-identity mechanism. IMPLICATION for Paper 2 positioning: PAV/PROV-O materially weakens any claim that "distinguishing performer/reviewer/creator roles on a record" is itself a novel contribution -- that vocabulary problem is solved in the general Semantic Web literature. Paper 2's defensible novelty is not inventing a role taxonomy, but applying/adapting an established provenance vocabulary (PAV/PROV-O or equivalent) specifically to BIM-GUID-anchored inspection records with an explicit approval-state and negative-case validation protocol, which no paper in this corpus combines.
