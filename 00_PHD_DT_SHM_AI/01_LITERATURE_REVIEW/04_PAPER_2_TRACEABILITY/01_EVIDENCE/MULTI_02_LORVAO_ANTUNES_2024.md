# MULTI_02 -- Lorvão Antunes, Barateiro, Marecos, Petrović & Cardoso (2024)

**Ontology-based BIM-AMS integration in European Highways.** Intelligent Systems with Applications, 22, 200366. DOI: 10.1016/j.iswa.2024.200366. Zotero key: XUN8EX6X. Touches Group 2 (semantic data integrity) and Group 3 (inspection-record accountability); tagged CLOSEST_COMPETITOR by prior curation.

**Source read:** Full text, complete (`G1_XUN8EX6X_LorvaoAntunes.txt`, 14 pages, all sections including Discussion/Limitations/Conclusion).

**Q1 -- Research problem.** How to enable interoperability between Building Information Modeling (BIM) and Asset Management Systems (AMS) for European highway infrastructure (bridges, tunnels, pavements), so that sensor/inspection ("Risk and Condition") data captured operationally can be linked back to BIM/IFC model elements for decision support, via a purpose-built "Engineering Structures" OWL ontology (the CoDEC project).

**Q2 -- Information architecture (Source -> mechanism -> BIM/component -> record -> output chain).** YES -- explicitly built and demonstrated. CLAIM: the paper defines a chain: Sensor/Inspection Observation (via extended SSN/SOSA ontology, `sosa:usedProcedure`, `sosa:hasFeatureOfInterest`) -> Structural Element (`codec:Structural_Element`, a `eurotl:PhysicalObject` subclass) -> ifcOWL link to `ifcElement` -> `ifc:globalId_IfcRoot` (the IFC GUID) -> visual/decision-support output (element colorization by risk in a BIM viewer). EVIDENCE: SPARQL query for CQ8 (Listing 5, p.9 of extract) traverses exactly this chain: Observation -> hasFeatureOfInterest(element) -> hasRepresentation -> ifcOWL representation chain -> `ifc:globalId_IfcRoot` -> GUID string. LIMITATION: the chain terminates at "risk and condition indicator + IFC GUID," not at a full inspection-record object with performer/reviewer/approver metadata. IMPLICATION: this is the single closest architectural precedent to Paper 2's core chain (Sensor_ID -> BIM_GUID/IFC identity -> Condition_Evidence_ID) found in the entire targeted corpus, confirming the CLOSEST_COMPETITOR tag.

**Q3 -- Identity representation.** YES. Uses `ifc:globalId_IfcRoot` (standard IFC GUID) explicitly as the terminal identity anchor connecting ontology-level Structural Elements to their BIM representation (Fig. 5, "Connection between PhysicalObject to a ifcElement GUID").

**Q4 -- Identity persistence across model revisions.** NOT_REPORTED. CLAIM: the paper's Limitations section (Sec. 8.3) states that "the process of populating the ontology still depends on manual intervention, including... the mapping between the Engineering Structures ontology and the ifcOWL instances," and does not address what happens to this GUID-based mapping when the underlying IFC model is revised/re-exported. LIMITATION: no discussion of GUID stability, re-export drift, or versioning found anywhere in the text. IMPLICATION: this is a genuine, explicitly-acknowledged gap in the closest competitor paper -- directly supporting a remaining-gap claim for Paper 2 on identity persistence across revisions specifically (not merely single-snapshot linkage, which this paper does solve).

**Q5a -- Source provenance.** PARTIAL. Observations record `hasFeatureOfInterest` (which element) and are performed by an "Agent, taking the Sensor role" (p.7), i.e., a source/agent field exists for sensor observations, but not for inspection-derived Risk and Condition Results specifically beyond "Procedure = Inspection."

**Q5b -- Measurement provenance.** PARTIAL. `hasSimpleResult` gives a 1-5 numeric Risk and Condition Indicator; `hasResult` points to a descriptive state, due date, and photo URL -- but no explicit sensor-calibration, method, or measurement-uncertainty metadata is recorded.

**Q5c -- Processing provenance.** NOT_REPORTED. No discussion of how raw inspection findings are processed/aggregated into the stored indicator.

**Q5d -- Human-actor provenance.** NOT_REPORTED beyond the generic "Agent" concept for sensor Observations; no distinction of which human performed a bridge inspection.

**Q5e -- Review/approval provenance.** NO. CLAIM: no reviewer, approver, or superseded-record concept appears anywhere in the ontology description, competency questions, or demonstration. EVIDENCE: the 13 competency questions (Table 2) cover construction date, location, measurements, ownership, element composition, sensor observation counts, inspection results/risk, and pavement geometry -- none address who reviewed or approved an inspection result. IMPLICATION: this is a clear, confirmed remaining gap even in the closest-competitor paper, directly supporting Paper 2's G3 record-accountability novelty claim.

**Q6 -- Model/version context.** NOT_REPORTED. No explicit handling of "which IFC model version" a given Observation/inspection result refers to.

**Q7 -- Validity/applicability scope.** YES, explicitly stated. CLAIM: "Currently, knowledge of the ontology is limited to the primary structures associated with the CoDEC pilot projects (i.e. tunnels, bridges and pavements)... the full potential of the ontology remains to be explored. Standards such as SHACL or SWRL... have not been addressed in this research" (Sec. 8.3). EVIDENCE: directly quoted limitation. IMPLICATION: the paper self-limits its scope and explicitly flags SHACL-based validation as future/unaddressed work -- reinforcing that SHACL-based integrity validation (Group 2 of this review) remains an open technique-integration opportunity even for the closest competitor's architecture.

**Q8 -- Forward and reverse traceability.** PARTIAL. Forward traceability (Structure -> Elements -> Observations/Inspections -> BIM GUID) is demonstrated via SPARQL competency-question queries (CQ5, CQ8, CQ9, CQ10). Reverse traceability (from an IFC GUID back to all associated inspection/risk records) is implied by the same underlying graph structure but not separately demonstrated as a named use case.

**Q9 -- Validation method.** YES. Two explicit validation mechanisms: (1) competency-question answering via SPARQL, evaluated against the Ontology Requirements Specification Document; (2) formal ontology-quality validation using the OOPS! (OntOlogy Pitfall Scanner!) tool, which found no critical pitfalls, 55 "important" pitfalls (54 attributable to imported ontologies, not the paper's own ontology), and 9 minor pitfalls (3 own).

**Q10 -- Deliberate invalid-case testing.** NO. CLAIM: OOPS! validation checks for *authoring* pitfalls (e.g., undeclared equivalent classes, naming inconsistencies), not for *data-instance* invalid cases such as orphaned records, duplicate/wrong/stale GUIDs, mismatched components, or broken URIs. EVIDENCE: Section 6.2 describes only pitfall categories of this authoring-quality kind; no negative-case data-instance testing appears anywhere in the demonstration (Section 7) or evaluation (Section 6). IMPLICATION: this is a second confirmed remaining gap in the closest-competitor paper -- no deliberate invalid-reference/orphan/stale-GUID testing was performed, directly supporting a Paper 2 novelty claim around validation-protocol rigor (GAP-G/GAP-H).

**Q11 -- G3 record fields (performer/reviewer/approver/timestamp/status/superseded-record/evidence-source).** PARTIAL: timestamp (`prov:atTime`, used to find "the last risk analysis result," CQ10) and evidence-source (photo URL) are present; performer, reviewer, approver, explicit status enumeration, and superseded-record concepts are NOT_REPORTED / confirmed absent from the described ontology.

**Q12 -- ALREADY_ESTABLISHED vs REMAINING_GAP.** ALREADY_ESTABLISHED: (a) a working, demonstrated ontology-mediated chain connecting sensor/inspection observations to structural elements to IFC GUIDs, validated via competency questions and OOPS!, deployed across three real pilot projects (tunnels, bridges, pavements) for a European highway consortium; (b) SPARQL-based forward query traceability from structure to element to inspection result to GUID. REMAINING_GAP, explicitly confirmed by this paper's own text: (1) GUID/mapping stability across IFC model revisions is unaddressed; (2) SHACL/SWRL-based validation is explicitly named as unaddressed future work; (3) no deliberate invalid-case (orphan/duplicate/stale-GUID) testing was performed; (4) no reviewer/approver/superseded-record accountability layer exists. This is the strongest available "closest competitor" evidence that Paper 2's specific combination of (i) explicit identity-persistence-across-revision handling, (ii) SHACL-style negative/invalid-case validation, and (iii) multi-role record accountability (performer/reviewer/approver) is not yet closed by the nearest comparable published system.
