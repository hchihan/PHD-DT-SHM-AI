**Q1 -- Research problem.** China's Chinese Bridge Management System (CBMS, developed 1993) suffers from fragmented, isolated information storage, lack of visualization linking maintenance data to the bridge model, and poor collaboration between bridge engineers/managers. The paper develops a BIM-based Bridge Management System (BMS) that extends IFC and IFD standards for the Chinese bridge industry, integrates GIS, enables Web-BIM/portable-device access, and demonstrates the system on a real long-span cable-stayed bridge.

**Q2 -- Information architecture.** YES -- a full, real, deployed chain. CLAIM: inspector (mobile app, on-site) -> structural-defect data entry against a standardized defect-description template -> IFC-extended attribute sets (`Pset-diseasetype`, `Pset-reinforcetype` with boolean sub-properties like `Crack`, `HungrySpots`, `Spalling`, `Corrosion`, `Deformation`, and detailed crack attributes `CrackDirect`/`CrackLength`/`CrackWidth`) attached to bridge members via IFC relationship entities -> data center (cloud resource/access/storage/application layers) -> lightweight 3D bridge model (color-coded by evaluation score) -> repair/reinforcement record module. EVIDENCE: Tables 1-4 (attribute-set and attribute definitions), Figure 1 (IFC extension expression architecture), Figures 12-16 (inspection workflow, defect information module, evaluation module, repair-plan module). This is the most complete end-to-end sensor/inspection-to-BIM-to-record chain found among the G3-tagged papers in this corpus, deployed on a real structure.

**Q3 -- Identity representation.** PARTIAL. Bridge members are referenced as `IfcBuildingElement` (or subtypes, e.g. beam, abutment) with disease/reinforcement attribute sets attached via IFC relationship entities; the paper does not explicitly name `IfcRoot.GlobalId` as the identity mechanism used to key inspection/repair records to specific elements, though the underlying IFC entity model implies it is present. IMPLICATION: GUID usage is implicit rather than an explicitly engineered/discussed feature of the system.

**Q4 -- Identity persistence across model revisions.** NOT_REPORTED. No discussion of how element identity is maintained when the bridge model is updated over its long operational life; the case study describes ongoing use of a single established model with accumulating inspection/repair records, not model-revision scenarios.

**Q5a -- Source provenance.** PARTIAL. Defects are attributed to a specific "inspection" event and location, localized by selecting the bridge member on the lightweight 3D model on-site; the source (the specific inspector's device/session) is implied but not modeled as an explicit provenance field.

**Q5b -- Measurement provenance.** PARTIAL. The attribute-set schema captures structured defect metrics (e.g. `CrackLength`, `CrackWidth`, direction) and text/picture/video evidence, but no explicit measurement-instrument or calibration metadata is described.

**Q5c -- Processing provenance.** NOT_REPORTED. No description of how raw field-collected defect data is processed/validated before being written into the model.

**Q5d -- Human-actor provenance.** NO / NOT_REPORTED as an explicit schema field. CLAIM: the inspection workflow (Figure 12) describes inspectors logging in via mobile app, localizing the member, and submitting defect information, after which "managers can check it in the system" -- implying an inspector/manager distinction exists operationally -- but no named database field (e.g. `performedBy`) is shown in the attribute tables (Tables 1-4), and no explicit inspector-identity or manager-identity field is documented in the extracted text.

**Q5e -- Review/approval provenance.** PARTIAL. CLAIM: "managers can check it in the system" after inspectors submit defect information (Sec. 5.2.2), and after repair/reinforcement work, "one can determine whether the maintenance is qualified or not according to the actual situation... the repair results can be check[ed] by the managers" (Sec. 5.2.2, repair module) -- describing an operational manager-review step for both inspection submissions and repair completions. LIMITATION: this is described narratively as a workflow behavior, not documented as an explicit accountability schema field (no named "reviewer," "approved_by," or "status" attribute appears in Tables 1-4, which only formalize the *defect/reinforcement type* attribute sets, not the record-accountability metadata). IMPLICATION: the paper's real system very likely implements *some* review step operationally, but does not formalize or expose it as part of the IFC/IFD data model it documents -- a genuine, confirmed gap between operational practice and data-model formalization that Paper 2 can explicitly target (formalizing what this paper leaves implicit).

**Q6 -- Model/version context.** NOT_REPORTED.

**Q7 -- Validity/applicability scope.** YES, explicit and real. CLAIM: system deployed on the Beijing-Hangzhou Grand Canal Bridge, a real double-tower semi-floating cable-stayed bridge (464 m main span: 108+248+108 m) plus 11-span approach bridges, classified "second level" condition with documented real defects (shrinkage cracks on tower/main beams, deformed cable-anchorage protective covers, longitudinal cracks on approach-bridge box girders, damaged laminated rubber bearings). Modeled in Autodesk Inventor, lightweight-processed for Web-BIM display, integrated with GIS.

**Q8 -- Forward and reverse traceability.** PARTIAL. Forward: selecting a bridge member (e.g. via the model tree, "pile foundation of thirteenth span," Figure 11) surfaces its static information, drawings, and attributes. Reverse/evidence linkage: the repair-and-reinforcement module lets users "view the relevant information on the repair plan" and "the location of maintenance" is displayed through the model (Figure 16) -- i.e., from a repair record back to its physical location/component. No explicit "list all inspection records for GUID X" query is described as such, though the underlying architecture (model layer "correlates the information such as inspection, evaluation, and maintenance reinforcement in the database with the component of the bridge") implies it is supported.

**Q9 -- Validation method.** PARTIAL/YES -- real deployment with qualitative feedback, not a controlled comparison study. CLAIM: "After this maintenance system was built, it was applied to the company in charge of the maintenance of the Grand Canal Bridge. Feedback was quite positive." No quantitative validation metrics (accuracy, defect-detection rate, comparison against manual process) are reported -- this is an operational deployment case study, not a controlled validation experiment.

**Q10 -- Deliberate invalid-case testing.** NOT_REPORTED. No discussion of orphan-record, missing-ID, duplicate, or broken-reference testing anywhere in the paper.

**Q11 -- G3 record fields (performer/reviewer/approver/timestamp/status/superseded-record/evidence-source).** performer -> PARTIAL (inspector role implied operationally, not a documented schema field); reviewer -> PARTIAL (manager-check step described narratively for both inspection and repair submissions, not formalized as a schema field); approver -> NOT_REPORTED as distinct from reviewer; timestamp -> NOT_REPORTED explicitly in the attribute tables (though system-level logging is plausible); status -> PARTIAL (defect-severity color-coding -- green/yellow/red -- functions as a de facto status indicator for structural condition, but not for record-workflow status); superseded-record -> NOT_REPORTED; evidence-source -> YES (the unstructured-data layer explicitly includes "Documents, Database, Picture & videos" attached alongside structured defect attributes, per Figure 1).

**Q12 -- ALREADY_ESTABLISHED vs REMAINING_GAP.** ALREADY_ESTABLISHED: a real, deployed, IFC/IFD-extended BMS attaching structured defect/reinforcement attribute sets (with quantitative crack metrics and multimedia evidence) to bridge elements via IFC relationship entities, with GIS integration, Web-BIM/portable-device access, and an operational (if informally documented) inspector-submit / manager-review workflow for both inspections and repairs, deployed on a real cable-stayed bridge with documented real structural defects. This is strong evidence that "inspection/repair records attached to BIM elements via IFC extension, deployed on a real structure" is established territory. REMAINING_GAP, directly relevant to Paper 2: (1) the inspector/manager-review workflow described operationally in the text is NOT formalized as an explicit data-model schema field (no `performedBy`/`reviewedBy`/`status` attribute in the paper's own attribute-definition tables) -- this is a concrete, citable example of the gap between "review happens in practice" and "review is a first-class, queryable, auditable record field," which Paper 2 can explicitly target as its contribution; (2) no GUID-persistence-across-revision discussion; (3) no deliberate invalid-case/negative testing; (4) the paper's own stated limitation is that "the BIM model cannot be connected to the finite element model (FEM), which strongly limits the structural analysis ability" and that "data in the system are not mined deep enough" -- explicitly out of scope for Paper 2's traceability focus but worth noting as the authors' own acknowledged future-work boundary.
