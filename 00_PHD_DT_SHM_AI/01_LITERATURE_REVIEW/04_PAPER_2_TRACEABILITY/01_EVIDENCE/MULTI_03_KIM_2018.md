# MULTI_03 -- Kim, Kim, Kim, Kim, Kim & Yu (2018)

**Integration of ifc objects and facility management work information using Semantic Web.** Automation in Construction, 87, 173-187. DOI: 10.1016/j.autcon.2017.12.019. Zotero key: VI5QMSAE. Touches Group 2 (semantic linkage/data integrity) and Group 3 (inspection/work-record accountability); tagged CLOSEST_COMPETITOR by prior curation.

**Source read:** Full text, complete (`G3_VI5QMSAE_KimEtAl.txt`, 15 pages).

**Q1 -- Research problem.** BIM-based facility information generated during design/construction is difficult to access and reuse in the operation-and-maintenance (O&M) phase because it is not linked to FM work-order/maintenance-record databases; the paper proposes an OWL ontology to semantically link IFC objects and COBie data to historical FM maintenance work records so facility managers can retrieve related past work by BIM object.

**Q2 -- Information architecture.** YES -- explicitly built and demonstrated. CLAIM: chain is IFC-based BIM model (IfcSpace, IfcBuildingElement entities) + COBie data sheet -> extracted via a six-layer architecture (IFC extraction layer, COBie exchange layer, FMS data-management layer, ontology-management layer, reasoning layer, query layer) -> OWL/RDF-bound "FM ontology" -> SPARQL query layer -> retrieved historical maintenance work records keyed by IFC object ID. EVIDENCE: Table 4 shows extracted IFC building-element concepts (entity type, IFC ID such as `#32465`, relatedType, relatedName, finMaterial); Table 5/Table 6 show maintenance work records retrieved by querying a given space's or element's associated IFC object IDs (e.g., IfcDoor `#66803`, IfcWallStandardcase `#45712, #45623, #45534`). LIMITATION: the "record" retrieved is a flat maintenance-work-log row (date, area, elements, work contents, laborers, work time, floor) with no explicit performer/reviewer/approver role split. IMPLICATION: second-closest architectural precedent to Paper 2's core chain after XUN8EX6X (Lorvão Antunes et al.), but keyed to internal IFC entity IDs (`#32465`) rather than to the persistent `IfcRoot.GlobalId`/GUID specifically -- a materially different identity mechanism.

**Q3 -- Identity representation.** PARTIAL. CLAIM: the paper keys its extracted "IFC ID" field to the internal EXPRESS/STEP line-number-style entity ID shown in the extraction algorithm (e.g., `#88`, `#103`, `#135` for Building/Storey/Space; `#32465`, `#63713` for building elements), not explicitly to `IfcRoot.GlobalId`. EVIDENCE: Tables 3-4 and the extraction algorithms (Figs. 7-8) reference "IFC ID" without naming GlobalId as the identifier field used. LIMITATION: this distinction is significant -- STEP entity line numbers (`#nnn`) are *not* guaranteed stable across re-exports of the same model, unlike `IfcRoot.GlobalId`, which is the identity mechanism Paper 2's core chain is built on. IMPLICATION: if this paper indeed uses raw STEP IDs rather than GUIDs (as the extracted tables suggest), it represents a *weaker* identity mechanism than Paper 2's proposed chain, and does not resolve or even engage with the GUID-persistence-across-revision question -- reinforcing rather than closing this remaining gap.

**Q4 -- Identity persistence across model revisions.** NO / NOT_REPORTED. CLAIM: the Discussion (Sec. 5) states "BIM models are seldom updated in FM practice, in terms of the hardware required to run the BIM authority software... most facility managers lack the skill and experience to manage a BIM model," implying the paper's own case study assumes a largely static as-is BIM model rather than tracking identity across successive revisions. EVIDENCE: explicit statement in Sec. 4.2.2 (Operation) about BIM models rarely being updated in practice. LIMITATION: no persistence mechanism, GUID-based or otherwise, is proposed or tested for the case where the BIM model *is* revised. IMPLICATION: this closest-competitor paper explicitly flags, but does not solve, the same identity-persistence-across-revision problem central to Paper 2.

**Q5a -- Source provenance.** NOT_REPORTED. No explicit encoding of which external source (e.g., which FM company system) a given work record originated from beyond the fact that five FM companies' historical records were collected for evaluation (Table 1).

**Q5b -- Measurement provenance.** NOT_APPLICABLE -- maintenance work records are text descriptions of work performed, not sensor measurements.

**Q5c -- Processing provenance.** NOT_REPORTED. No description of how raw FMS-DB work-log entries are transformed/validated before ontology population beyond "converted to RDF data" via Jena API.

**Q5d -- Human-actor provenance.** NO. CLAIM: Table 2's maintenance-record schema captures date, area, elements, work contents, number of laborers, working time, and floor -- but not the identity of the person who performed or recorded the work. EVIDENCE: Table 2 column headers explicitly enumerated; no "performed by" or personnel-identity field appears anywhere in the ontology description (Figs. 2-4, 10).

**Q5e -- Review/approval provenance.** NO. No reviewer, approver, or record-status/superseded-record concept appears anywhere in the paper.

**Q6 -- Model/version context.** NOT_REPORTED. The case study uses a single "common BIM file - office" sample model (buildingSMART reference file) at Level 1/Level 2; no multi-version scenario is modeled or discussed.

**Q7 -- Validity/applicability scope.** YES. Explicitly scoped: evaluated using 590 historical occasional-maintenance work records from five Korean FM companies, applied to a two-story sample office IFC model (60 spaces, 262 walls, 66 doors, 25 windows). Limitations (Sec. 6/Conclusion) explicitly note the approach requires further validation of the handed-over BIM model (including COBie) and further work on updating actual conditions of existing buildings.

**Q8 -- Forward and reverse traceability.** YES (demonstrated, though not in Paper 2's terminology). CLAIM: SPARQL queries retrieve, for a given space or shared building element (e.g., a wall or door shared between adjacent spaces 139 and 140), all historical maintenance records referencing that element -- a form of reverse traceability from BIM component to work-record history (Table 5, Table 6). Forward traceability (from a work record to its associated BIM element) is implicit in the same bound RDF graph.

**Q9 -- Validation method.** YES. Case-study validation: 590 real maintenance-work records from 5 Korean FM companies bound via Jena 2.6.4/Protégé 3.4.4 to IFC/COBie RDF data, reasoned over with the BOSSAM reasoner, queried via SPARQL/ARQ; results compared against the traditional (non-BIM, keyword-search-only) FM process, reporting an approximately 200% increase in retrieved related-work-record quantity when adjacent shared-element records are included.

**Q10 -- Deliberate invalid-case testing.** NOT_REPORTED. No mention of missing-ID, orphan-record, duplicate-ID, stale-reference, or broken-link negative testing anywhere in the evaluation (Sec. 4).

**Q11 -- G3 record fields (performer/reviewer/approver/timestamp/status/superseded-record/evidence-source).** NO for performer/reviewer/approver/status/superseded-record; PARTIAL for timestamp only (`Date` column in Table 2, e.g., `20160115`). No evidence-source (e.g., photo/document reference) field is present, in contrast to XUN8EX6X's photo-URL field.

**Q12 -- ALREADY_ESTABLISHED vs REMAINING_GAP.** ALREADY_ESTABLISHED: ontology-mediated linkage of IFC/COBie building elements to historical FM work records, with real multi-company case-study validation and quantified retrieval-quality improvement (~200% more related records surfaced via shared-element linkage). REMAINING_GAP, directly relevant to Paper 2: (1) the identity mechanism used appears to be internal STEP entity IDs rather than the persistent `IfcRoot.GlobalId`, so GUID-specific identity persistence is not engaged with at all; (2) the paper itself flags that BIM models are rarely updated/revised in FM practice, sidestepping rather than solving the cross-revision identity problem; (3) no human-actor, reviewer/approver, or record-status/supersession fields exist in the maintenance-record schema; (4) no deliberate invalid-case/negative testing was performed. This further corroborates that a GUID-anchored, multi-role-accountable, negative-tested traceability chain (Paper 2's proposed contribution) remains open even against the closest identified competitors.
