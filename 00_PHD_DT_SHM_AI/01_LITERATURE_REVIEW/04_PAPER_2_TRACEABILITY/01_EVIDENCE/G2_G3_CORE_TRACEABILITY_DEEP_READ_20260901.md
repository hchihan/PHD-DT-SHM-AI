## 2. Piras et al. (2026) - `P3YLFNJZ`

**Citation:** Piras, G., Rossini, F. L., Muzi, F., & Sagayaraj, M. (2026). An open standard methodology for BIM-CMMS integration: Enhancing facility operations through IFC-based data enrichment. *Applied Sciences, 16*(10), 4642. https://doi.org/10.3390/app16104642

| Extraction item | Full-text finding | Locator |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | The paper addresses the handover/interoperability break between design-oriented BIM/IFC and operational CMMS data. Its specific target is a lightweight, reproducible, vendor-neutral, post-export workflow that enriches IFC according to an owner's information requirements and populates an open-source CMMS without proprietary middleware. | PDF pp. 2-9, Sections 1.1-1.6; especially p. 9, final paragraphs. |
| Method/framework | Design-science case study with four main stages: OIR-derived information requirements; as-built Revit models; Python/IfcOpenShell injection of a custom `ConstantElementProperties` Pset; buildingSMART validation and OpenMAINT 2.4 import through a JSON mapping template. The Pset includes `Condition`, `Criticality`, `Installation_Date`, `Last_Check_Date`, `Maintenance_Frequency`, `Manufacturer`, and `Model_ID`; missing text/numeric values use `N/A`/`999`. | PDF pp. 10-20, Section 2; pp. 13-15, Tables 2-3 and Algorithm 1; pp. 18-19, Table 4 and Figure 4. |
| Unit of analysis | Two existing residential buildings in Bologna; 12 discipline-specific models; about 9000 IFC elements processed; a selected subset of 428 maintainable assets registered as CMMS cards. The operative identity unit is the asset element/card: `IfcGlobalId` is mapped to the CMMS unique asset identifier, while `Model_ID` is a separate asset reference code. The paper does not define an inspection-record or work-order instance as the unit tested. | PDF pp. 10-11, Section 2.1; p. 18, Section 2.4.2 and Table 4; pp. 20, 22-24, Tables 5-7. |
| Verification design | Positive-path, multi-layer technical verification: buildingSMART checks STEP syntax, IFC4 schema, inverse attributes, type constraints, and cardinality; three viewers inspect Pset readability; OpenMAINT import reconstructs hierarchy and cards; representative assets receive sample-based field comparisons. The paper reports no failed import, duplicate GUID, orphan, or broken link in the tested data, but does not seed controlled invalid cases or preregister an expected violation set. | PDF pp. 17-24, Sections 2.4 and 3.1, Figure 3, Tables 5-7. |
| Main results | All 428 maintainable assets were registered; mapped fields and hierarchy were reported intact; average enrichment was about 4 min/model, CMMS population less than 3 min/building, file size rose 2.5%, and manual effort was estimated to fall about 85%. `IfcGlobalId` supports one-to-one asset/card correspondence and bidirectional visual navigation between the CMMS list and 3D viewer. Work orders can be associated with model components as a platform capability, but no instantiated work order, execution outcome, or closure transaction is reported. | PDF pp. 20-26, Sections 3-3.3; p. 20 Table 5; p. 22 Table 6; p. 23 Table 7; pp. 24-25 Sections 3.2.2-3.3.1; p. 26 Section 3.3.2. |
| Limitations | `AUTHOR-STATED`: the 85% value is a case estimate, not a general benchmark; no formal comparator, error/stability/sensitivity analysis, or varying-information robustness test; the workflow is BIM-to-CMMS only; custom Pset reuse requires project-specific mapping; operational data are static and no IoT/BMS stream is integrated. `READER-INFERRED`: all core validation is positive-path and schema/field oriented; placeholder values can satisfy field completeness without evidential validity; no record-level actor, authorization, immutable event history, action execution, or post-action verification is tested. | PDF pp. 20-21, Section 3 and Table 5; pp. 27-30, Sections 4.1-4.3. |
| Future work | API/IFC transaction mechanisms for controlled CMMS-to-IFC write-back; standardization of FM Psets; ontology/middleware linkage to live IoT/BMS events; expansion of attributes and facility types. | PDF pp. 29-31, Section 4.3 and Section 5. |
| Claim boundary | **Allowed:** open-standard post-export IFC enrichment, stable asset anchoring by `IfcGlobalId`, lossless sampled field transfer, 428 asset cards, and visual navigation in both directions were implemented. **Not allowed:** calling the viewer interaction transactional bidirectional synchronization, source-target-source round-trip parity, lifecycle reconstruction, or verified closure. The paper explicitly states that write-back of maintenance completion, inspection outcomes, and replacements is absent. Its phrases “complete synchronization”, “continuous information flow”, and “digital twin” do not override that explicit limitation. Work-order association is capability evidence, not evidence of an executed `Action_Record`. | PDF pp. 24-26, Sections 3.2.2-3.3.3; pp. 27-30, Sections 4.1-4.3; pp. 30-31, Section 5. |

## 3. Li et al. (2023) - `YY8TP9ZT`

**Citation:** Li, S., Zhang, Z., Lin, D., Zhang, T., & Han, L. (2023). Development of a BIM-based bridge maintenance system (BMS) for managing defect data. *Scientific Reports, 13*, 846. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-27924-6

| Extraction item | Full-text finding | Locator |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Conventional bridge maintenance systems store large defect inventories mainly in 2D and do not efficiently combine visualization, standardized defect data, condition evaluation, and maintenance recommendations. The paper targets daily defect management rather than an SHM-only system with long inspection intervals. | PDF pp. 1-2, Introduction. |
| Method/framework | Web-based standalone BMS developed through prototyping plus a waterfall lifecycle; HTML/CSS/JavaScript front end and PHP/MySQL back end. It combines a four-category BIM3D defect library, IFD-derived classification/coding, a defect database, multi-level bridge-condition scoring, normative four-level maintenance recommendations, and a Revit/IFC 2x3/OBJ visualization pipeline. | PDF pp. 2-8, Methods; p. 3 Figures 1-2; pp. 4-5 Tables 1-3; pp. 5-7 Figures 5-6 and Table 4; p. 8 Section “Utilization...”. |
| Unit of analysis | One 1767 m Cocodala Bridge case. Information is organized at defect, component, assembly, bridge-part, and whole-bridge levels. A new defect receives an incremented prefix plus classification code, e.g. `1-68-10 10 10`; inspection staff enter scale, location, description, and image. The record form visibly contains recorder/reviewer fields, but the text does not define authenticated role enforcement or review events. | PDF p. 3, defect-ID examples; pp. 5-7, scoring hierarchy; pp. 7-8, case and inspection-data collection; p. 11, Figure 12. |
| Verification design | Functional case application: build/import the bridge and defect models, upload twice-weekly inspection data, link a selected defect model to the corresponding bridge location through an ID attribute in MySQL, calculate standard-based scores, visualize colour grades, and display maintenance advice. | Main results | The system implements defect visualization, a structured defect database, bridge-condition scoring, and maintenance-priority recommendations. Solved defects are marked `completed`, and repair records are stored for later maintenance options. This is stronger than a recommendation-only concept, but the paper does not expose a separate stable repair/action identifier, completion timestamp/event, actor, authorization, or relation to an acceptance/reinspection record. | PDF p. 7, “Recommendations...” and Table 4; pp. 8-13, utilization, Discussion, and Conclusion. |
| Limitations | `AUTHOR-STATED`: Revit-to-OBJ transformation removes native parameters and makes mapping limited/inefficient; the BMS remains at an early functional stage; coded manual inspection cannot accurately locate defects; the system lacks structural analysis. `READER-INFERRED`: only one bridge is demonstrated; database schema/cardinality/version rules are not published; generated defect IDs are illustrated but uniqueness across imports/versions is not tested; `completed` is a scalar current marker and repair records are mentioned without an event/verification schema. | PDF p. 13, paragraph before Conclusion; PDF pp. 2-8 for implemented scope. |

## 5. Hagedorn, Pauwels & König (2023) - `II3UWN2C`

**Citation:** Hagedorn, P., Pauwels, P., & König, M. (2023). Semantic rule checking of cross-domain building data in information containers for linked document delivery using the shapes constraint language. *Automation in Construction, 156*, 105106. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2023.105106

| Extraction item | Full-text finding | Locator |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | ICDD can link heterogeneous documents/entities, but the quality of metadata and cross-domain links is difficult to assess. Existing model checking largely targets a single IFC/domain model. Native ICDD link structures, link types, and string/URI identifiers also make SHACL rules long and hard to write. | PDF pp. 2-3 [article pp. 1-2], Abstract and Introduction. |
| Method/framework | Three validation layers are distinguished: ICDD schema validation, domain-data validation, and link validation. The paper develops an ICDDL ontology whose SHACL-SPARQL `CONSTRUCT` rules infer direct object predicates between linked RDF entities and generate inverse relations. Four ICDD 4D datasets combine IFC models, XML schedules, and links; three link types and three increasingly complex SHACL rules are benchmarked. | PDF pp. 6-10 [article pp. 5-9], Section 3, Figures 5-9, Listing 1, Tables 1-2. |
| Unit of analysis | RDF focus nodes/links between building elements and construction tasks inside four ICDD containers. Dataset sizes span 9-832 elements, 8-115 tasks, 9-959 binary links, and 202-27,816 linkset triples depending on link type. The study unit is a building-element/task relation and its graph representation, not an inspection, maintenance action, or asset lifecycle. | PDF pp. 9-10 [article pp. 8-9], Section 3.2.2 and Table 2. |
| Verification design | JMH microbenchmark on one Windows/JDK machine; five warm-up and 20 measured iterations. It compares inference, validation on inferred graphs, and validation on original graphs. Rules are: R1 every element has at least one task; R2 tasks linked to the same element do not overlap; R3 load-bearing reinforced-concrete wall tasks do not exceed a performance coefficient. Rules were manually prepared and evaluated with an industry partner, but are explicitly benchmark-specific rather than universally applicable. | PDF pp. 7-10 [article pp. 6-9], Sections 3.2-3.2.3; p. 10 R1-R3. |
| Main results | The inference creates forward and inverse triples; directed 1-to-n links reduce triple counts about 24-49% in these datasets. Inferred graphs simplify rule writing and usually reduce validation runtime; combined inference-plus-validation benefits appear mainly in larger datasets. Validation finds R1 violations `0/30/39/1`, R2 `0/0/0/0`, and R3 `0/0/7/0` across datasets 1-4. Reported validation stays below about 2.5 s. | PDF pp. 11-17 [article pp. 10-16], Sections 4-5, Figures 10-12 and Tables 3-4; especially PDF p. 16 [article p. 15], Table 4. |
| Limitations | `AUTHOR-STATED`: no RDF indexing; not a comparison of SHACL implementations; findings depend on processor, datasets, rules, links, and identifier choice; string-based identifiers are awkward beyond L1 properties; some linked IFC entities absent after RDF conversion yield no inferred triples; inference overhead can outweigh savings for small data. `READER-INFERRED`: violations are present in benchmark data but are not documented as isolated seeded mutations with a preregistered expected focus-ID/code/count oracle; no inspection/action/status/authorization/closure semantics, transactional write-back, or source-target-source reconstruction is tested. | PDF pp. 8-10 [article pp. 7-9], Sections 3.2-3.2.3; pp. 11-17 [article pp. 10-16], Sections 4-5. |
| Future work | Compare more SHACL implementations; add RDFS/OWL reasoning and examine order/performance; improve ICDD linking for payload documents and RDF entities; use RDF indexing; test other ICDD use cases; consider SHACL-JS and software implementation usability. | PDF p. 17 [article p. 16], final paragraphs of Section 5. |
| Claim boundary | **Allowed:** SHACL can validate cross-domain ICDD links; the implementation infers direct and inverse RDF predicates and executes missing-link/temporal/performance rules on four datasets. **Not allowed:** calling inverse-predicate generation transactional bidirectional synchronization, lifecycle reverse traceability, round-trip parity, or exact inverse-edge certification. The benchmark validates element-task links only and does not establish the canonical thesis chain or a maintenance closure process. | PDF pp. 6-7 [article pp. 5-6], Figures 5-6 and Listing 1; pp. 15-17 [article pp. 14-16], Section 4.3, Table 4, and Conclusion. |

**Direct gap effect:** Hagedorn materially weakens Gap 3. Generic cross-domain referential checking, missing-link detection, and direct/inverse relation inference or traversal are implemented prior art and cannot be claimed independently. If Gap 3 is written only as “integrated referential integrity plus bidirectional traceability,” it is too broad and is weak against this paper. The defensible remainder must explicitly require chain-specific rules and exact expected-versus-actual forward/reverse result sets or source-target-source semantic parity across the unchanged canonical chain; Hagedorn does not execute that contract.

### 6.1 Gap 2 verdict

**Current exact gap:** “Chuỗi truy vết tích hợp, có định danh, từ condition evidence tới action đã kiểm chứng.”

**After full-text audit:** `NARROWED_BUT_DEFENSIBLE_AS_A_CONJUNCTION_GAP`.

Piras reaches from IFC assets and OIR-derived FM attributes to CMMS cards with stable `IfcGlobalId`; Li reaches from human inspection and generated defect IDs to condition scores, maintenance recommendations, stored repair records, and `completed`. These sources directly weaken broad absence language. Gap 2 would be false if phrased as “no stable BIM-CMMS identity,” “no BIM-linked inspection/defect record,” “no repair record,” or “no completion state.”

The residual gap remains because neither work combines the unchanged canonical `Sensor_ID → BIM_GUID → Condition_Evidence_ID → Inspection_Record_ID → Action_Status` chain with a provenance-complete evidence basis, explicit governed status history, accountable human review, and formal post-action verification/guarded closure. This is a corpus-bounded conjunction finding, not a universal first/absence claim. Literature evidence does not freeze G2.

### 6.2 Gap 3 verdict

**Current exact gap:** “Toàn vẹn tham chiếu tích hợp kết hợp truy vết hai chiều.”

**After full-text audit:** `GENERIC_FORM_WEAK; CHAIN_SPECIFIC_EXACT_ORACLE_FORM_NARROWED_BUT_DEFENSIBLE`.

Hagedorn is a direct counterexample to a generic claim: it validates cross-domain ICDD links, detects missing element-task links, and infers direct plus inverse predicates. Piras independently establishes two-way CMMS/3D-viewer navigation anchored by `IfcGlobalId`. Therefore, generic “referential integrity + bidirectional links/retrieval” cannot remain the novelty unit.

The residual Gap 3 must be limited to the approved G3 acceptance contract: exact expected/actual paired forward-reverse result sets; rejection of orphan, duplicate, stale/reused ID, wrong type, and invalid cardinality; evidence, chronology, authorization, and closure failures; and implementation-neutral exact-oracle equality across the unchanged canonical chain. Neither Piras, Li, nor Hagedorn executes source-target-source reconstruction with declared normalization/equivalence or exact chain-level oracle assertions. Nuyts cannot be used to refine this conclusion in the present pass because full text was not available in Zotero.
