# 10. Act as a critical reviewer and challenge this proposed research contribution:

A verifiable component-level traceability framework linking
vibration-based condition evidence to BIM objects, inspection records,
and action status.

Search specifically for peer-reviewed studies that already implement
all or most of this chain:

raw sensor data
→ derived condition evidence
→ BIM/IFC component identifier
→ inspection or maintenance record
→ action or work-order status.

For each closest competing study, provide:

- DOI and verified publication metadata;
- exact links in the chain that were implemented;
- validation method;
- whether competency questions or reproducible criteria were used;
- which links remain absent;
- whether the study is building, bridge, industrial asset, or another domain.

Do not defend the proposed PhD gap. Identify the strongest competing papers.

This critical review found **no peer-reviewed study implementing the full five-link chain** (raw vibration data → derived condition evidence → BIM/IFC identifier → inspection record → action/work-order status) with competency-question validation — but several partial competitors deserve direct engagement rather than a dismissive "no one has done this" claim.

**Strongest partial competitor:** Deng et al. (2022, *JAABE*) reaches sensor → threshold-based condition flag → BIM Element ID → warning record for a bridge, but uses simple threshold logic (not leakage-controlled classification) and never reaches a tracked action-status object.[^1]

**Earliest sensor-to-BIM precedent:** Rio et al. (2013) embedded structural sensors directly into the IFC schema for a real instrumented building — proving BIM-sensor linkage is *not* novel by itself, only the specific combination the PhD proposes.[^2]

**Two unverified leads that could weaken the gap claim:** Bellon et al. (2025, *Automation in Construction*) explicitly frames "IFC framework for inspection and maintenance representation," and Xu et al. (2024) covers IFC-based defect-to-analysis-model transfer — both require full-text deep reads before the thesis gap statement can be confidently finalized.[^3][^4]

**Key critique of the proposed contribution:** Its novelty should rest specifically on pairing leakage-controlled, classifier-derived vibration evidence with a competency-question-validated BIM-inspection-action schema — not on BIM-sensor linkage generally, which has prior art back to 2013, nor on BIM-inspection linkage, which has prior art in bridge defect modeling since at least 2018.
