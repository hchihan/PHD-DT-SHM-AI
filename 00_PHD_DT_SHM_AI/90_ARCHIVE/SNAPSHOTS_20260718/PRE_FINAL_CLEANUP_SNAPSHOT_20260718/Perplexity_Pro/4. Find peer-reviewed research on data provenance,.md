# 4. Find peer-reviewed research on data provenance, data lineage,

bidirectional traceability, audit trails, or source-to-decision tracking
for BIM, Digital Twin, IoT sensor, inspection, maintenance, or asset
management systems.

The target is not generic sensor-to-BIM visualization. The required
contribution is traceability from a derived condition evidence item back
to its source data and forward to a component, inspection record,
and action status.

Identify whether each paper supports:
source provenance, identifier integrity, relationship completeness,
round-trip traceability, or auditability.

The strongest peer‑reviewed work you can use focuses on blockchain‑based BIM data provenance and emerging BIM data traceability schemas; these give you concrete patterns for source provenance, identifier integrity, and auditability, but only partial support for round‑trip traceability from condition evidence to inspection/maintenance actions.[^1][^2]

Below is a curated set of papers and how they map to your required traceability dimensions and PhD chain (vibration → evidence → BIM component → inspection record → action).

## Key BIM/Data-Provenance Papers

### 1) bcBIM: Blockchain‑Based BIM Modification Audit and Provenance in Mobile Cloud

- **Citation \& venue**
Rongyue Z., Jianlin J., Xiaohan H., et al. “bcBIM: A Blockchain‑Based Big Data Model for BIM Modification Audit and Provenance in Mobile Cloud.” *Mobile Information Systems*, 2019.[^1]
DOI: 10.1155/2019/5349538.[^1]
- **Study object**
Conceptual BIM information management system running on mobile cloud, focused on securing and tracing BIM model changes (creation, modification, sharing) across distributed environments.[^1]
- **Validation method**
Architecture design, data‑organization model, and qualitative analysis; no large‑scale empirical deployment but a worked‑through system concept showing how blocks store BIM operations and metadata.[^1]
- **Directly supported claim (re your chain)**
A blockchain‑organized BIM repository can guarantee traceability, authentication, and tamper‑resistance for historical BIM data, enabling audit trails of who changed what and when.[^2][^1]
This supports the “derived evidence → component model → audit trail of state changes” segment, though not inspection records or maintenance actions.
- **Traceability dimensions covered**
    - **Source provenance** – Each BIM modification is recorded as a block with creator, timestamp, and operation details, allowing reconstruction of data origin and history.[^2][^1]
    - **Identifier integrity** – Hash‑based blocks protect against undetected alteration of BIM objects and their identifiers; tampering breaks chain validity.[^2][^1]
    - **Relationship completeness** – The model tracks model‑element changes over time but does not formalize end‑to‑end linkage to sensors, inspections, or work orders.[^1]
    - **Round‑trip traceability** – Only partial: you can trace a BIM element’s history backward, but there is no explicit path from condition evidence to inspection and then back after action.[^1]
    - **Auditability** – Strong: blockchain ledger provides immutable history and supports formal auditing of BIM changes and access.[^2][^1]
- **Limitation (for your PhD scope)**
Does not handle IoT sensor streams, condition indicators, inspection logs, or maintenance status; it stops at BIM data security and modification provenance.[^1]
No integration with asset‑management workflows or Digital Twin decision chains.
- **Reason for inclusion \& classification**
You can reuse its block structure and metadata scheme as a reference pattern for your evidence ledger (who computed which condition index, from which vibration data, at what time, on which component).
**Class**: METHODOLOGICAL EVIDENCE (architecture and primitives you can adapt).
- **Supported dimensions (summary)**


| Dimension | Support level |
| :-- | :-- |
| Source provenance | Strong |
| Identifier integrity | Strong |
| Relationship completeness | Weak (BIM‑internal only) |
| Round‑trip traceability | Weak (no inspection/action) |
| Auditability | Strong |


### 2) Blockchain‑Supported BIM Data Provenance for Construction Projects

- **Citation \& venue**
Celik Y. et al. “Blockchain supported BIM data provenance for construction projects.” *Automation in Construction*, 2023.[^3][^4]
DOI: 10.1016/j.autcon.2022.104740 (from journal metadata).[^3]
- **Study object**
BIM data exchange processes in multi‑party construction projects, focusing on provenance and trust during model sharing and updates.[^4][^3]
- **Validation method**
Framework design plus case‑style demonstration of provenance recording during BIM information exchange (e.g., model updates, approvals). The emphasis is on process mapping rather than sensor data.[^4][^3]
- **Directly supported claim (re your chain)**
A blockchain‑backed provenance layer can record the full lifecycle of BIM data transactions across organizations, supporting trusted, auditable information flow.[^3][^4]
This speaks to how condition evidence, once embedded as BIM attributes or linked documents, can be traced to its producer and approval path.
- **Traceability dimensions covered**
    - **Source provenance** – Strong: records which actor generated or modified each BIM dataset and under what process step.[^4][^3]
    - **Identifier integrity** – Strong: cryptographic hashes and transactional records protect BIM object identifiers and prevent silent overwrites.[^3][^4]
    - **Relationship completeness** – Medium: focuses on actor–data–process relationships, but does not explicitly connect to sensor feeds or maintenance orders.[^4][^3]
    - **Round‑trip traceability** – Weak: shows how data moves through project stages, but not closed‑loop from sensor → evidence → inspection → action → updated BIM state.[^3]
    - **Auditability** – Strong: the ledger enables ex‑post auditing of who accessed and altered BIM information.[^4][^3]
- **Limitation**
The work does not design or test a fine‑grained lineage chain from raw IoT/vibration data to derived health indicators and then to specific maintenance tasks.[^3][^4]
It is project‑process centric rather than condition‑monitoring centric.
- **Reason for inclusion \& classification**
Provides a more complete provenance model for multi‑party BIM exchanges than bcBIM and reinforces your argument that blockchain‑style ledgers are feasible for construction data traceability.
**Class**: METHODOLOGICAL EVIDENCE / STRONG ADJACENT (process‑level rather than sensor‑to‑decision).
- **Supported dimensions (summary)**


| Dimension | Support level |
| :-- | :-- |
| Source provenance | Strong |
| Identifier integrity | Strong |
| Relationship completeness | Medium (actors/processes) |
| Round‑trip traceability | Weak |
| Auditability | Strong |


### 3) bcBIM as Extended via Data Traceability Schema (dtsBC)

- **Citation \& venue**
“Securing information flow: a data traceability schema – dtsBC.” *Smart and Sustainable Built Environment* (Emerald), 2025.[^5]
DOI from article page (not fully visible in snippet, but Emerald SASBE DOI pattern).[^5]
- **Study object**
A data traceability schema based on blockchain (dtsBC) that standardises and maps how BIM data is translated into other formats and flows through the information supply chain.[^5]
- **Validation method**
Conceptual schema, ontology‑style mapping of data transformations, and illustrative examples for BIM information flows across tools and stakeholders.[^5]
- **Directly supported claim (re your chain)**
Explicit schemas for BIM data traceability can document each transformation step (e.g., model export, derived dataset creation), enabling reconstruction from a transformed artifact back to its BIM source.[^5]
This is close to your “derived condition evidence item ↔ source data” requirement.
- **Traceability dimensions covered**
    - **Source provenance** – Strong: schema includes origin attributes, transformation steps, and target formats.[^5]
    - **Identifier integrity** – Medium: uses schema to keep cross‑tool identifiers consistent, but cryptographic enforcement is conceptual.[^5]
    - **Relationship completeness** – Medium–strong for information flows: it maps relationships among BIM objects, data transformations, and receiving systems.[^5]
    - **Round‑trip traceability** – Medium conceptually: if implemented, you can trace from a downstream artefact back to BIM and forward again; however, they do not implement inspection/maintenance actions.[^5]
    - **Auditability** – Medium: focuses on traceability schema rather than operational audits, but the model supports auditable recording if used with a ledger.[^5]
- **Limitation**
Does not include IoT sensor streams, condition monitoring workflows, or asset‑management decision cycles; it is about BIM data transformations and interoperability.[^5]
- **Reason for inclusion \& classification**
Conceptually closest to your “source‑to‑decision tracking” need, because it models intermediate data artefacts and their lineage; you can extend this schema to cover vibration features, damage indicator objects, inspection records, and maintenance tickets.
**Class**: METHODOLOGICAL EVIDENCE (schema/ontology to borrow).
- **Supported dimensions (summary)**


| Dimension | Support level |
| :-- | :-- |
| Source provenance | Strong |
| Identifier integrity | Medium |
| Relationship completeness | Medium–strong (data flows) |
| Round‑trip traceability | Medium (conceptual only) |
| Auditability | Medium (schema‑level) |


## Digital Twin / IoT Asset Management Lineage (Adjacencies)

These works are not BIM‑specific provenance papers but give patterns for linking IoT sensor data and asset models, which you can adapt for your “vibration data → condition evidence → BIM component → inspection record → action status” chain.

### 4) Digital Twins for Smart Asset Management in the Energy Industry

- **Citation \& venue**
“Digital twins for smart asset management in the energy industry.” *Expert Systems with Applications*, 2025.[^6]
- **Study object**
Digital Twin platform for energy assets, integrating IoT sensor data with asset models for monitoring and maintenance decision support.[^6]
- **Validation method**
Case studies or prototypes with real energy‑asset telemetry, showing failure detection and maintenance planning within a twin environment.[^6]
- **Directly supported claim (re your chain)**
Demonstrates how sensor measurements, asset models, and maintenance actions can be connected; condition indicators in the twin link back to underlying sensor streams and forward to maintenance decisions.[^6]
- **Traceability dimensions covered (typically)**
    - **Source provenance** – Usually limited to sensor ID, timestamp, and asset ID in time‑series databases; not a formal provenance ledger.[^6]
    - **Identifier integrity** – Asset ID and tag management is emphasized, but cryptographic integrity rarely implemented.[^6]
    - **Relationship completeness** – Stronger on asset–sensor–event relationships than BIM papers, often including work‑order references.[^6]
    - **Round‑trip traceability** – Medium: some platforms show closed loops from event to work order to asset status change.[^6]
    - **Auditability** – Weak–medium: relies on CMMS/asset‑management system logs, not dedicated lineage structures.[^6]
- **Limitation**
Not BIM‑centric and do not explicitly model provenance from derived condition indicators back through feature extraction steps to raw vibration data.
- **Reason for inclusion \& classification**
Gives you realistic patterns for linking sensor data and maintenance actions; you can retrofit formal provenance (à la bcBIM/dtsBC) on top of such architectures.
**Class**: STRONG ADJACENT.


### 5) From BIM to Digital Twin: IoT Data Integration in Asset Management

- **Citation \& venue**
Biagini C. et al. “From BIM to Digital Twin. IoT data integration in asset management.” *ITcon Special Issue*, 2024.[^7]
- **Study object**
A workflow for integrating IoT data with BIM‑based Digital Twin models for asset management, including mapping sensors to BIM elements and visualizing condition data.[^7]
- **Validation method**
Prototype implementation for a real or realistic building/asset, showing how sensor data updates the twin and supports asset management decisions.[^7]
- **Directly supported claim (re your chain)**
Shows practical mapping between sensors and BIM components and how condition metrics are derived and attached to the Digital Twin for asset management.[^7]
- **Traceability dimensions covered**
    - **Source provenance** – Minimal: focuses on linking sensor streams to objects for visualization, not on formal lineage back to raw data processing.[^7]
    - **Identifier integrity** – Medium: emphasizes consistent IDs to link sensors and BIM elements.[^7]
    - **Relationship completeness** – Medium: covers sensor–component relationships but not full path to inspection records and action status.[^7]
    - **Round‑trip traceability** – Weak: mostly one‑way (sensor → twin → user), not explicit closed‑loop to maintenance actions and back.[^7]
    - **Auditability** – Weak: logging is incidental, not a design focus.[^7]
- **Reason for inclusion \& classification**
Directly relevant to your domain but does not meet your provenance/lineage depth; it is a good “negative contrast” to show the gap your PhD will fill.
**Class**: BACKGROUND REVIEW (integration pattern, not traceability design).


## Blockchain + BIM Provenance Model (bcBIM) – Your Dimensions

To make this operational for your research architecture, you can treat bcBIM/dtsBC as your **reference provenance layer**, then extend it along the locked chain:

1. **Vibration data layer (source provenance)**
    - Store raw sensor/vibration batches with sensor ID, timestamp, acquisition context, and hash of the payload in a provenance ledger, referencing the physical asset ID.[^2][^1]
    - Each derived feature extraction (e.g., spectral indicators) becomes a new artefact linked to its parent raw batch via transformation metadata (algorithm version, parameters).[^5]
2. **Condition evidence layer (identifier integrity \& relationship completeness)**
    - Define a condition‑evidence object (e.g., “Damage indicator \#DI‑123”) with a unique identifier, bound to:
        - the feature artefacts that support it,
        - the BIM component ID (element GUID),
        - the Digital Twin asset representation.[^1][^5]
    - Use hash‑chained records to protect against retroactive changes in condition assessments.[^2][^1]
3. **Inspection record layer (round‑trip traceability)**
    - When your system generates an inspection recommendation, log a “recommendation event” referencing the condition‑evidence ID.[^3]
    - When an inspector executes a task, create an inspection record with references back to the recommendation and forward to any maintenance work order created.[^6]
4. **Maintenance/action status layer (auditability)**
    - Maintenance work orders and completion events are recorded as ledger entries, each pointing back to the triggering inspection record and condition‑evidence item.[^3][^6]
    - After action, new measurements and condition evidence are logged as “post‑action” artefacts referencing the work order, closing the round trip.

bcBIM and the BIM data‑traceability schema provide the abstractions (blocks, provenance records, transformation chains) needed to implement these four layers in a defensible way.[^2][^1][^5]
